One night with Dreamfeather

Lucid dreams mostly happen in REM sleep, which gets longer toward morning. Each tool fits a moment of the night, so you only do what helps, when it helps.

  1. Lights out

    Bedtime in one tap

    Pick a wake time, an optional sleep sound and the wake-back-to-bed alarm. Breathe with the moon while it grows and shrinks, then put the phone down.

  2. While you sleep

    A red screen that keeps your eyes dark

    Red light barely disturbs melatonin, so the night screen glows red, never white. Woke up? Log a fragment or pause tracking without flooding the room with light.

  3. About 4½ hours in

    Wake back to bed

    The classic lucid dreaming technique, timed for you. A gentle alarm wakes you a few hours after you fall asleep. Stay up a few minutes, then go back to bed. The second sleep is mostly REM, which is where lucid dreams start.

    It rings over the lock screen with big snooze and stop buttons you can hit half asleep.

  4. Back under the covers

    Guided MILD, read aloud

    Replay the dream you just had, spot what was strange, and repeat your intention with each breath. Dreamfeather speaks every step so you can follow with your eyes closed. SSILD is there too, if you prefer it.

  5. First light

    Catch the dream before it fades

    Write or dictate it the moment you wake. Mark it lucid, rate your recall and tag the people, places and situations that keep coming back. Patterns counts the words that recur across your dreams, right on your phone.

  6. Through the day

    Reality checks and honest history

    Gentle reminders at random times ask you to check whether you are dreaming, naming your own dream signs. History shows your nights, dreams and lucid rate by week and month, with streaks and badges along the way.

    Wear a watch? Health Connect can import its nights and export the ones you log.

Questions, answered

What is lucid dreaming?

A lucid dream is a dream in which you realise you are dreaming while it is still happening. Once you notice, you can often stay in the dream, look around calmly or steer what happens next. Most lucid dreams happen during REM sleep, which gets longer in the last hours of the night.

Does the wake-back-to-bed technique work?

Wake back to bed combined with MILD is the best-supported beginner method. In a small lab study, Erlacher and Stumbrys (2020) reported lucid dreams in about 53% of nights with WBTB plus MILD, against 9% in a reading control group. A 2022 replication found a weaker effect when wake timing shifted, so treat that number as an upper bound rather than a promise.

Does Dreamfeather need an account or the internet?

No Dreamfeather account is needed. Sleep logging, typed journaling and alarms work offline. Google Play purchases and ownership checks need connectivity and use your Play account. Optional dictation and read-aloud voices may use a provider's online services. Records are stored on your phone; you can export files or connect Health Connect yourself. The privacy policy explains purchases, speech, exports and deletion.

Does Dreamfeather measure my sleep stages?

No. You tap start when you lie down and wake when you get up, and the app times the wake-back-to-bed alarm from that. It does not guess sleep stages from phone sensors. Dreamfeather is not a medical device and is not meant to diagnose or treat any sleep condition.
